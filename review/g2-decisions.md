## G2-01 — Policy for requests exceeding available power

- **Disposition:** Confirmed by the user, 6 November 2026; D001 policy **Open → Agreed**.
- **User confirmation:** “confirm”.
- **Question:** should a test profile that exceeds the available combined or individual rail budget be rejected before enabling that profile, with revised settings requiring an explicit request?
- **Agreed decision:** reject infeasible requests and report the conflicting limits. Reserve power locally for the profile's declared simultaneous operating envelope, including bounded current demand, rather than relying on a nominal or initially measured load. Keep the requested operating point unchanged; an alternative profile must be explicitly selected and revalidated.
- **Reason:** automatically reducing heater or electrode settings changes the conditions under which the valve is measured. Explicit rejection preserves the meaning of the requested test and makes the power tradeoff visible.
- **Example:** under the current Provisional ceiling, a 60 W heater allocation plus a 90 W anode allocation totals 150 W: 30 W above 120 W before screen or grid demand. The profile must be revised; neither allocation is silently reduced. This example proves arithmetic only and does not accept a continuous rating or usable command ceiling.
- **Alternatives:** visible priority-based derating, or fixed power modes. The former needs a defined priority and clear reporting of changed test conditions; the latter limits the available combinations. Neither is selected.
- **Scope of this decision:** admission policy for a requested profile and explicit local power reservation. Numeric reservations, grid source/sink behavior, accounting boundary/window, guard margin, live-change sequencing and fault thresholds still require subsequent decisions. Independent local protection remains mandatory; protective limiting during a test must not be reported as successful operation at the unchanged requested conditions.
- **Trace / next evidence:** D001; R026,R030–R036,R039,R050,R053. R036 and R053 remain Agreed, with R053 expanded to capture the confirmed admission/reservation behavior. Verify accepted/rejected profiles, explicit reporting of constraints, revalidation after a changed request, and local enforcement without relying on the PC. Detailed protection behavior remains with D004/D013/D014 and the safety review.
- **Baseline / gate impact:** baseline revision 0.3 §4 records the agreed policy; revision 0.2 is archived verbatim. Requirement statuses and numerical limits are unchanged. D001 and D019 are Agreed; 20 detailed decisions remain Open. G1 remains passed; G2 remains in progress, not passed. No hardware mechanism, fault timing or numerical operating envelope is approved.

## G2-02 — Combined output-power accounting boundary

- **Disposition:** Confirmed by the user, 6 November 2026; D004 accounting boundary **Open → Agreed**, with D004 overall still **Open**.
- **User confirmation:** “confirm”.
- **Question:** define the combined output ceiling at the valve/load connection terminals, or at the supply-module outputs before routing losses?
- **Agreed decision:** use the valve/load connection terminals at the adapter/fixture as the delivery boundary, including heater and all electrode/test-stimulus power delivered into that circuit. Count each supplied power contribution once, including when it is shared between the valve and a resistive load. Assign upstream converter, wiring and matrix losses to the separate input-power and heat budgets. Controls, relays, cooling and any microphonics actuator are auxiliary loads, not additional electrode/heater output; their input and heat still require explicit budgeting. The separate full-load/PIV fixture exclusions remain unchanged.
- **Reason:** ties the output rating to power available for the test. Routing and conversion losses must be supplied and cooled in addition to delivered output, so the ceiling cannot also serve as the mains-input rating.
- **Alternative:** a module-output boundary includes downstream routing losses in the combined ceiling and consequently leaves less of that ceiling available at the valve/load. It may simplify measurement placement, but would require the worksheet and delivery specifications to use that convention consistently.
- **Scope / implementation left Open:** this selects the accounting boundary only, not sensor placement or a measurement circuit. Exact port/reference definitions, directly heated filament accounting, returned-power treatment, averaging window, tolerances, trip margins, individual reservations and the numerical ceiling remain for subsequent review. This decision does not approve net or gross power accounting.
- **Trace / verification:** D004; R035–R038, R068. Review the complete delivered-power, loss and auxiliary ledgers for omitted or double-counted paths; later verify delivered power, routing losses and input demand against independent measurements under the approved waveform and sign conventions.
- **Baseline / gate impact:** baseline revision 0.4 records the agreed boundary and removes it from the unresolved wording in the G2-01 paragraph; revision 0.3 is archived verbatim. R035 retains its Provisional numerical ceiling while recording the Agreed boundary, R037 retains Agreed status with the explicit loss/auxiliary convention, and R038 remains Open for load-bank specifications. ## G2-03 — Returned energy and combined output demand

- **Disposition:** Confirmed by the user, 6 November 2026; no returned-energy credit and separate absorption limits are **Agreed** within D004. D004 overall remains **Open**.
- **User confirmation:** “Yes, confirm no returned-energy credit, with separate absorption limits. Record it and walk me through the next decision.”
- **Question:** should energy returned from the valve/load reduce the combined output allocation, or should supplied and absorbed energy have separate limits?
- **Agreed decision:** count all power delivered into the valve/load circuit toward the combined output budget, with no credit for returned energy on any port or during other parts of the measurement window. Limit returned power/energy separately against the relevant absorption path's capabilities and heat budget. This policy does not require or select a regenerative supply.
- **Accounting expression of the policy:** at each agreed delivery port, define signed instantaneous power as positive into the valve/load. Over a future defined window, use `P_budget = Σ average(max(v_j(t) × i_j(t), 0))`. Track returned power separately as `P_return = Σ average(max(−v_j(t) × i_j(t), 0))`, with per-port peak/energy/thermal limits still to be specified. A negative voltage alone does not identify returned power; current direction also matters. The window, measurement implementation and exact port definitions remain Open.
- **Illustration:** 110 W supplied while 5 W returns still counts as 110 W against the output budget; the 5 W return has a separate absorption requirement. This is accounting arithmetic, not an approved operating point, sink rating or power allowance.
- **Reason:** avoids making an output allocation depend on assumed recovery or dissipation of returned energy. Each source and absorption path must meet its own limits even when signed powers partly cancel. This is conservative for reactive waveforms and may allow less test power than a validated net-power scheme.
- **Alternative:** net-power accounting subtracts returned energy from delivered energy. It would need a reviewed recovery architecture, cross-rail accounting and local constraints that still bound individual source/sink paths and transients. No such implementation has been selected or validated.
- **Details still Open:** grid quadrants and continuous/peak ratings (D003), signed port/reference conventions including directly heated filaments (D009), window/response time, sensing uncertainty, margins, numeric reservations, stored-energy limits, thermal model and protection mechanism (D004/G3). The 120 W ceiling remains Provisional; no averaging window or numerical sink rating is approved. Actual input/heat calculations must follow physical energy paths without double counting; the gross allocation convention is not itself a complete loss or regenerative-flow model.
- **Trace / verification:** R034–R037,R050 and D003/D004/D009. Later verify sourcing, sinking and mixed-port waveforms, incorrect sign handling, returned-energy overloads and the independence of per-rail and aggregate protection.
- **Baseline / gate impact:** baseline revision 0.5 records the policy and removes returned-energy treatment from the earlier unresolved boundary paragraph; revision 0.4 is archived verbatim. R034 remains Open for numeric grid ratings, R035 remains Provisional for the numerical ceiling, R036/R037 remain Agreed. All 80 requirement statuses are unchanged. D001/D019 are Agreed and 20 decisions, including D004, remain Open overall. G1 remains passed; G2 and later gates remain unpassed.

## G2-06 — Fan-assisted cooling or a fanless requirement

- **Disposition:** Confirmed by the user, 7 November 2026; temperature-controlled fan-assisted cooling is the **Agreed preferred approach** within D006. D006 overall remains **Open**.
- **User confirmation:** “Confirm fan-assisted cooling as the preferred approach, with noise, fault limits and hardware details still Open. Record it and walk me through the next decision.”
- **Question:** allow temperature-controlled fan-assisted cooling as the preferred design approach, or require a fully fanless main unit?
- **Agreed decision:** allow heatsinks with temperature-controlled fan-assisted cooling as the preferred approach for continuous operation through 40 °C ambient. Determine actual airflow, heat paths and whether any operating regions can be fanless from the loss/thermal analysis. This is an architectural preference, not a selected fan, controller, control circuit, heatsink or proven need for airflow at every operating point.
- **Rationale / tradeoff:** keeping forced airflow available gives the thermal design another way to meet the continuous envelope. Temperature-based speed control can reduce acoustic noise compared with always running at full speed; [Analog Devices discusses this tradeoff and drive-related acoustic effects](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/technical-articles/circuit-converts-pwm-fan-drive-to-linear-and-reduces-acoustic-noise.html) (SRC35). Fan power, acoustic noise, vibration, electrical interference and service needs must be budgeted. No numerical noise improvement is assumed.
- **Alternative:** require passive cooling throughout the approved envelope. This avoids fan-generated noise/vibration and fan wear, but needs a demonstrated passive heat path at 40 °C, potentially with larger heatsinks/chassis or different conversion-loss targets. No reduced output rating or duty cycle is silently accepted to make either approach fit.
- **Measurement / protection constraints:** preserve the Agreed audio, low-noise and microphonics capabilities; assess fan operation across its speed range for mechanical and electrical measurement contamination. No fan-off measurement window is promised before its thermal/noise limits are demonstrated. Local protection must handle relevant cooling failures independently of the PC; sensor/fan monitoring, thresholds, fault timing and post-fault cooling remain for D006/D013/D014 review.
- **Details still Open:** heat distribution, enclosure, airflow paths, acoustics/vibration limits, fan count/size/type/control method, power/duty, life/service plan, sensor placement, loss/fault response and load-fixture cooling. The user's explicit reservation of noise, fault limits and hardware details is retained. This preference does not establish thermal feasibility or close D006.
- **Trace / verification:** new R082 records this Agreed preference and retained measurement/protection constraints; D006,D010,D013,D014,D022 and R023–R025,R030,R035–R038,R049–R051,R081 remain linked. Analyze worst-case continuous heat at 40 °C; later measure thermal equilibrium, audio/microphonics contamination and relevant blocked-airflow/fan/control/sensor faults within reviewed setups. Numeric acceptance criteria precede testing. I020, K04/K06 and P-E/P-M/P-T carry the interface, risk and prototype implications without selecting hardware.
- **Baseline / gate impact:** baseline revision 0.8, dated 7 November 2026, records the preference; revision 0.7 is archived verbatim. R082 is new and Agreed with owner/source/verification; original R001–R081 statuses and numerical limits are unchanged. D006 remains among 20 Open decisions overall. G1 retains its pass with the coverage update for this existing D006 question; G2/later gates remain unpassed. No cooling hardware or numerical noise/fault limit is selected.

Each path still needs terminal-voltage readback and local limits. A common setpoint leaves the supply and return topology open.

Independently adjustable anode voltages would also allow simultaneous comparison at two different plate voltages, at the cost of regulating and verifying two distinct setpoints. This choice concerns anode voltage only. Screen control and the allowed current distribution have their own open decisions under D002 and D018.
